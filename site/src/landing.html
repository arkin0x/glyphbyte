The alphabet

Sixteen pictures. Four dots. That's all.

Each glyph is one picture plus up to four corner dots, which makes 256 different glyphs. Six in a row are enough to tell one link apart from trillions of others. Pick a picture and tap the corners.

Drawing

Draw a row that reads.

Neat handwriting is not needed. The reader cares about six things, and they all come naturally after one row.

  1. Square framesAll about the same size along the row.
  2. Picture upright in the middleAbout half the frame wide and touching nothing. Keep the box and the x small.
  3. Dots in the cornersFilled blobs about a sixth of the frame across, clear of the frame and the picture.
  4. A gap between framesRoughly a third of a frame.
  5. An underlineOne stroke a little below the row and past both ends. It tells the reader which way is up.
  6. A fat start dotA quarter of a frame across, where the row begins. Without it a row reads both ways.

Dots touch nothing

A dot that touches the frame becomes part of the frame, and the glyph changes.

Points stay pointy

The roof of the house, the points of the crown and the corners of the triangle are what tell them apart from the box.

The start dot is fat

A thin dot looks like the end of the line. Make it obviously bigger than the corner dots.

How links are found

No company in the middle.

When you make glyphs, GlyphByte saves your link or note as a cairn: a tiny public record on nostr, an open network of independent servers called relays. The six glyphs are the start of that record's id, which is all the reader needs to find it. Nobody can change or take down a cairn once it is made, including you, so check it first.

When a glyph is unclear the reader keeps every likely reading and looks them all up at once. Wrong readings match nothing, so the right link still comes back. Chalk on rough pavement is harder: grainy strokes break frames apart, and whole rows drop to 45%. Rows in coloured chalk on a sidewalk read exactly. Faint chalk and rows in two colours are the next things to improve. Details in BENCH.md.

Build with it

Open, small and dependency-free.

GlyphByte is an open specification with 256 test vectors and a reader with no dependencies that runs anywhere a canvas does. Under the hood a row is the first bytes of a nostr event id or public key, looked up on relays that answer id prefixes, so the reader also opens nostr profiles and notes, and is itself a single-file napplet.
