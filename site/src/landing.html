    How it works

    Draw it. Snap it. Find it.

    A row of glyphs is the first few bytes of an event id or a pubkey. That is enough for a relay to find the one thing you meant.

    01

    Draw

    Take the first six bytes of an id. For each byte draw a square, the icon for its first hex digit in the middle and the dots for its second in the corners. Underline the row and start the line with a fat dot.

    02

    Snap

    Take a photo with the reader. It finds the frames, straightens them and reads every icon and dot with a small neural network that runs on your phone. Sideways and upside down photos read the same.

    e8ed3798c6fffound
    e8ed3798ceffnothing
    03

    Find

    Where a glyph is unclear the reader keeps every likely reading and asks a relay for all of them at once. Wrong readings match nothing, so the right one stands out. You pick what you meant.

    The alphabet

    Sixteen icons. Four dots. Every byte.

    The icon is the first hex digit. The dots add up to the second: 8 top-left, 4 top-right, 2 bottom-right and 1 bottom-left. Pick an icon and tap the corners.

    Drawing

    Draw a row that reads.

    Any pen works. The reader cares about six things, and they all come naturally after one row.

    1. Square framesAll about the same size along the row.
    2. Icon upright in the middleAbout half the frame wide and touching nothing. Keep the box and the x small.
    3. Dots in the cornersFilled blobs about a sixth of the frame across, clear of the frame and the icon.
    4. A gap between framesRoughly a third of a frame.
    5. An underlineOne stroke a little below the row and past both ends. It tells the reader which way is up.
    6. A fat start dotA quarter of a frame across, where the row begins. Without it a row reads both ways.

    Dots touch nothing

    A dot that touches the frame becomes part of the frame, and the byte loses a bit.

    Points stay pointy

    The roof of the house, the points of the crown and the corners of the triangle are what tell them apart from the box.

    The start dot is fat

    A thin dot looks like the end of the line. Make it obviously bigger than the corner dots.

    Six bytes is the usual length for an event id or a pubkey. Use eight for something meant to last for years, and four only on a small relay.

    Where it goes

    Leave a pointer in the world.

    A drawn row is a link with no domain to rent and no code to print. Anything that holds a mark can hold a nostr id.

    {{icon:map-pin}}

    Walls and sidewalks

    Chalk six glyphs and anyone who passes can open the note behind them. Coloured chalk reads best.

    {{icon:sticker}}

    Stickers and signs

    A row fits on a sticker, a poster or the back of a business card, drawn by hand or printed.

    {{icon:notebook-pen}}

    Notebooks and letters

    Write someone's pubkey in a margin and the reader turns it back into their profile.

    {{icon:tractor}}

    Any size at all

    Glyphs have no fixed scale. A row works the same in a margin, on a garage door or mown into a field.

    Cairns

    Point a place at any link.

    Paste a link into the reader and it publishes a cairn: a small public nostr event that carries the link. You get six glyphs to draw. Whoever reads them sees the link and its domain before anything opens.

    Honest numbers

    How well it reads today.

    Measured on 200 synthetic scenes of pen and marker rows with messy handwriting, perspective, any camera angle, poor light, blur and photographed backgrounds.

    89%
    of drawn frames found
    95%
    of bytes read right when every frame was found
    63%
    of whole rows right as the first answer, before the relay check
    0
    photos leave your device. Only the relay query does.

    The browser reader scores within a point of the full Python model. With chalk on pavement and paths in crop fields mixed in, whole rows drop to 45% because grainy strokes break frames apart. Real rows in coloured chalk on a sidewalk read exactly, found by their colour. Faint chalk that differs from the ground only in colour, and rows in two colours, are the next things to improve.     Build with it

    Open, small and dependency-free.

    The specification comes with 256 test vectors. The JavaScript reader has no dependencies and runs anywhere a canvas does. The whole reader is also a single-file napplet that a nostr shell can load.
